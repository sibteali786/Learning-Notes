Neural Networks for Computer Vision

How a face photo becomes a million-number input vector, and why the same hidden-layer architecture that predicted T-shirt demand learns to detect edges, then face parts, then whole faces — with zero human labeling of any of it.

Turning a photo into a feature vector

A 1000×1000 pixel grayscale image is really a 1000×1000 matrix of pixel-intensity values (0–255). To feed it to a network, the matrix is "unrolled" row by row into one long column vector x⃗.

1000 × 1000 = 1,000,000 pixel values → one input vector x⃗

What the hidden layers learn, unsupervised

Feed that million-number vector through several hidden layers to an output neuron that predicts "probability this is person XYZ." Nobody tells any hidden neuron what to look for — but when researchers visualize what each layer's neurons respond to, a clear hierarchy emerges:

Edges

◠ ◡

Layer 3
☺

The receptive field grows with depth

Layer-1 neurons look at small patches of the image (just enough to see a short edge). Layer-2 neurons look at a bigger window (enough to see an eye or nose). Layer-3 neurons look at bigger still (enough to see a face shape). Each layer builds on the previous layer's output, so its effective "field of view" over the original pixels keeps expanding — simple local patterns compose into complex global ones.

Same architecture, different data → different features

Trained on faces

  • Layer 1: edges
  • Layer 2: eyes, noses, ears
  • Layer 3: face shapes
  • Output: probability of being person "XYZ"

Trained on cars

  • Layer 1: edges (nearly identical to the face model)
  • Layer 2: wheels, windows, car parts
  • Layer 3: complete car shapes
  • Output: probability a car is detected

The network isn't hard-coded to look for eyes or wheels — it's the training data that decides what each layer's activations end up representing. This is the same idea as the T-shirt example's hidden layer learning "affordability" and "awareness" on its own, just at a much larger scale and with a much richer hierarchy of learned features (called activations, and higher layers hold progressively higher-level features).

Key terms

TermMeaning
UnrollingFlattening a 2D pixel matrix into a single 1D feature vector x⃗ by reading it row by row.
Receptive field / windowThe region of the original image a given neuron's activation is effectively sensitive to; it grows with each successive hidden layer.
Hierarchical featuresSimple features (edges) in early layers composing into complex features (parts, then whole objects) in later layers.
Higher-level featuresWhat later-layer activations represent — more abstract and closer to the concept being predicted than raw pixels.
