The Setup

A is 2×3, so think of it as 3 column vectors: a1, a2, a3.

W is 2×4, so think of it as 4 column vectors: w1, w2, w3, w4.

2  -2  0.2
A (2×3)
3  5  7  9
4  6  8  0
W (2×4)

AT is formed by laying each column of A on its side, becoming rows: row 1 = a1T, row 2 = a2T, row 3 = a3T → shape 3×2.

The General Rule: Row × Column

Z[row i][col j] = (row i of AT) · (column j of W)

Every single entry of the output matrix Z is its own dot product between one row of AT and one column of W.

The video poses this as a self-check: given Z's position, identify which row of AT and column of W to dot together. Pattern: row index of Z → row of AT, column index of Z → column of W.

The Shape-Compatibility Rule

Core requirement: you can only take a dot product between two vectors of the same length.

E.g. [0.1, 0.2] (length 2) can dot with [5, 6] (length 2) — but not with a length-3 vector.

AT is 3×2     W is 2×4     →     inner dimensions (2 and 2) must match
RuleExplanation
Compatibility# columns of AT must equal # rows of W (both = 2 here) — so every row/column pair being dotted has matching length
Result shapeZ's rows = AT's rows (3); Z's columns = W's columns (4) → Z is 3×4
⚠️ Easy mistake

The "inner" numbers (columns of first matrix, rows of second) must match for the multiplication to even be valid — trying to multiply a 3×2 by a 3×4 would fail. The "outer" numbers (rows of first, columns of second) become the output's shape.

🎯 Real-world reading

This shape rule is exactly why shape-mismatch errors happen in Keras/TensorFlow — if your input's feature count doesn't match a layer's expected input dimension, the underlying matrix multiplication is literally invalid, and TensorFlow throws a shape error. Understanding this rule turns a cryptic error message into something you can immediately diagnose.
