📉 The K-Means Cost Function ("Distortion")

Why K-means always converges — and what it's actually minimizing

Notation — What x⁽ⁱ⁾, μₖ, and μ_c⁽ⁱ⁾ Mean

This is the part that confuses most people at first — here's a concrete worked example with 3 labeled points.

SymbolMeaningExample
x⁽ⁱ⁾The i-th training example itself (its raw features)x⁽¹⁰⁾ = the 10th data point, say (7, 3)
c⁽ⁱ⁾Which cluster index (1..K) point i is currently assigned toc⁽¹⁰⁾ = 2 → point 10 belongs to cluster 2 (blue)
μₖThe location (centroid) of cluster k, for any kμ₁ = red centroid, μ₂ = blue centroid, say μ₂ = (6, 4)
μ_c⁽ⁱ⁾The centroid of whichever cluster point i was assigned to — a lookup, not a fixed indexSince c⁽¹⁰⁾=2, μ_c⁽¹⁰⁾ = μ₂ = (6, 4)
Read μ_c⁽ⁱ⁾ as: "first look up which cluster point i belongs to (via c⁽ⁱ⁾), then go get that cluster's centroid." It's indirection, not a new variable.

The Cost Function (Distortion)

J(c⁽¹⁾,...,c⁽ᵐ⁾, μ₁,...,μ_K) = (1/m) Σᵢ₌₁ᵐ ‖x⁽ⁱ⁾ − μ_c⁽ⁱ⁾‖²

In words: average squared distance between every point and the centroid it's currently assigned to. Also called distortion.

min over c⁽¹⁾,...,c⁽ᵐ⁾, μ₁,...,μ_K   of   J(...)
K-means jointly searches over assignments c AND centroid positions μ to make this number as small as possible.

Step 1 Reduces J — by Choosing c⁽ⁱ⁾

Holding centroids fixed, each point's own term ‖x⁽ⁱ⁾ − μ_c⁽ⁱ⁾‖² is smallest when it's assigned to whichever centroid is physically closest.

Assigning x⁽ⁱ⁾ to the far centroid (μ₁) → large squared distance. Assigning to the near one (μ₂) → small squared distance → lower J. So step 1 always picks the nearer one.

Step 2 Reduces J — by Moving μₖ to the Mean

Holding assignments fixed, the numeric example from the lecture: 2 points on a line, at x=1 and x=11.

Centroid at x = 2 (bad guess)
J = 41
½(1² + 9²) = ½(1+81) = 41
Centroid at x = 6 (the mean)
J = 25
½(5² + 5²) = ½(25+25) = 25
Moving the centroid from x=2 to the mean x=6 (=(1+11)/2) drops J from 41 → 25. Any other location scores worse than the mean — the mean is mathematically the minimizer of average squared distance.
This is exactly why step 2 computes an average: the mean is the unique point that minimizes the sum of squared distances to a set of points.

What Is J Actually Reducing?

Step 1
Fixes μ, optimizes c → picks nearest centroid
Step 2
Fixes c, optimizes μ → moves to the mean
Result
J never increases, only ↓ or stays flat

Concretely, J is reducing the average squared "tightness" of each cluster — how far, on average, points sit from the centroid they're grouped under. Each of the two alternating steps is coordinate descent: optimize one set of variables (c, or μ) while freezing the other, then swap.

💡 Key Takeaways

J = avg squared distance to assigned centroid Step 1 & 2 = coordinate descent on J J monotonically decreases → guaranteed convergence
Practical use: if J ever increases, that's a bug. If J stops decreasing (or decreases very slowly), that's your convergence signal to stop. Next: using multiple random initializations to avoid bad local minima.
