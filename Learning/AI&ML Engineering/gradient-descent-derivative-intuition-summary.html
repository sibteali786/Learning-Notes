Why the Derivative Term Makes Gradient Descent Work DeepLearning.AI notes

Goal: Build intuition for why w := w − α · d/dw J(w) actually moves you toward the minimum, by examining the sign of the derivative term at two different starting points.

1. Simplifying to One Parameter

w := w − α · (d/dw) J(w)

Same idea as before (b=0 case) — with just w, J(w) can be drawn as a simple 2D curve, so the effect of each update is easy to see.

Note: what's technically a partial derivative (since J has multiple parameters in general) is casually called "derivative" for ML purposes — the distinction doesn't matter for implementing the algorithm.

2. What the Derivative Represents

At any point on the curve J(w), draw a tangent line — a straight line touching the curve at that exact point. The slope of that tangent line (rise ÷ run of a little triangle drawn against it) is the derivative d/dw J(w) at that point. It tells you which way the curve is sloping right at your current w.

3. Case A — Starting on the Right Side of the Minimum

Setup: starting w is to the right of the minimum. The curve slopes up and to the right there → tangent line has positive slope (e.g. 2/1 = +2).
Update math: w := w − α · (positive number) → subtracting a positive number → w decreases
Result: w moves left, toward the minimum. J(w) decreases. ✔ Correct direction.

4. Case B — Starting on the Left Side of the Minimum

Setup: starting w is to the left of the minimum. The curve slopes down and to the right there → tangent line has negative slope (e.g. −2/1 = −2).
Update math: w := w − α · (negative number) → subtracting a negative = adding a positive → w increases
Result: w moves right, toward the minimum. J(w) decreases. ✔ Correct direction, again.

5. The Pattern

Position relative to minimumSlope signUpdate effectDirection moved
Right of minimumPositive (+)w − α·(+) → decreasesLeft, toward minimum ✔
Left of minimumNegative (−)w − α·(−) → increasesRight, toward minimum ✔
In both cases, the derivative's sign automatically steers w in the direction that reduces J — that's the whole mechanism behind why gradient descent "knows" which way to move, without ever being told directly where the minimum is.

Real-World Framing

Example: Imagine standing on a slide (playground slide) that curves down into a pit. Wherever you stand, the local steepness under your feet tells you which way is "downhill from here" — you don't need a map of the whole slide, just the slope right where you're standing. That local slope is exactly what the derivative term gives gradient descent at each step.
