🧭 L4 — Process Inputs: Chain of Thought Reasoning

DeepLearning.AI · ChatGPT Prompt Engineering for Developers — Lesson 4 summary

🎯 The Problem: Models Rush to Conclusions

Left alone, a model can jump straight to an answer and make reasoning errors. Chain-of-Thought (CoT) prompting asks the model to work through explicit reasoning steps before giving a final answer — similar to how a person "shows their work."

Real-world scenario: A retail chatbot is asked to compare two product prices. Without CoT, it might just guess/assert an answer. With CoT, it's forced to look up each price and compute the difference step by step, catching a wrong customer assumption along the way.

🤫 Inner Monologue

For some apps (e.g. tutoring), showing the model's reasoning to the user is undesirable — it could give away the answer to a student. Inner monologue = ask the model to put reasoning into a structured, delimited format, then programmatically strip it out before showing the response to the user.

🏷️ 5-Step CoT Instruction Set

1Decide if user is asking about a specific product (not just a category).
2Check if the product(s) mentioned are in the store's 5-item list.
3List any assumptions the user is making (e.g. "X is bigger than Y").
4Check whether each assumption is actually true.
5Politely correct wrong assumptions; answer only using the 5 real products, in a friendly tone.

🧪 Worked Example: Model Catches a Wrong Assumption

User: "by how much is the BlueWave Chromebook more expensive than the TechPro Desktop?" (false premise — Chromebook is actually cheaper)

🙈 Hidden reasoning (steps 1–4 — not shown to user):
Step 1: comparing two specific products.
Step 2: both products exist in catalog.
Step 3: user assumes Chromebook is more expensive than Desktop.
Step 4: Desktop = $999.99, Chromebook = $249.99 → assumption is false; Chromebook is actually $750 cheaper.
👁️ Visible response to user: "The BlueWave Chromebook is actually $750 cheaper than the TechPro Desktop."

Second example — "do you sell TVs?" Model skips the assumption-checking steps (nothing applicable) and jumps straight to: "We currently do not sell TVs. Our store specializes in computers and laptops..." Note the model didn't perfectly follow the requested N/A step format here — a reminder that even with careful instructions, weaker/older models can deviate; newer models follow format more reliably.

✂️ Extracting Only the Final Answer (Code Pattern)

Since every step is separated by ####, splitting on the delimiter and taking the last segment isolates just the customer-facing response — hiding the inner monologue.

try:
    final_response = response.split(delimiter)[-1].strip()
except Exception as e:
    final_response = "Sorry, I'm having trouble right now, please try asking another question."

print(final_response)

Design note This whole 5-step prompt is intentionally shown as a somewhat convoluted example — the lesson explicitly calls out that simpler prompts may achieve the same result, and that finding the right complexity/reliability trade-off takes experimentation.

def get_completion_from_messages(messages, model="gpt-3.5-turbo", temperature=0, max_tokens=500):
    response = client.chat.completions.create(
        model=model, messages=messages,
        temperature=temperature, max_tokens=max_tokens,
    )
    return response.choices[0].message.content

Also worth knowing: newer reasoning-focused models (e.g. OpenAI's o-series / GPT-5-class reasoning models) do multi-step reasoning internally and often don't need this manual step-by-step CoT scaffolding — some even discourage exposing raw chain-of-thought. Worth checking current OpenAI guidance on reasoning models before porting this pattern to a newer model.
