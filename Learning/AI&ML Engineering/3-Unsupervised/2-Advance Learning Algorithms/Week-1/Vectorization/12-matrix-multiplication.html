Matrix Multiplication, Step by Step

Lesson 12 (optional) · Dot products → vector-matrix → matrix-matrix multiplication

1. Vector Dot Product — The Building Block

Given two vectors a = [1, 2] and w = [3, 4], the dot product multiplies matching elements and sums:

z = a · w = (1×3) + (2×4) = 3 + 8 = 11

Equivalent way to write the same thing: transpose a (lay it on its side, turning a column vector into a row vector), then multiply by w as a matrix:

z = aT w   (same result: 11)
FormShapeMeaning
a (column)2×1Standard vector
aT (row)1×2Transposed — elements laid sideways
aTw1×2 · 2×1 → scalarSame value as the plain dot product

This transpose-based way of writing a dot product is the key trick that generalizes to matrix multiplication.

Transpose A: take each column and lay it as a row:

AT = [ 1  2 ;  -1  -2 ]  — row 1 = a1T, row 2 = a2T

Key mental model: think of a matrix as its columns; think of its transpose as those columns turned into rows.

1Row 1 (a1T) × col 1 (w1): (1×3)+(2×4) = 11

2Row 1 (a1T) × col 2 (w2): (1×5)+(2×6) = 17

3Row 2 (a2T) × col 1 (w1): (-1×3)+(-2×4) = -11

4Row 2 (a2T) × col 2 (w2): (-1×5)+(-2×6) = -17

Z = ATW = [ 11  17 ;  -11  -17 ]   (shape 2×2)
RuleDetail
Result shape(rows of AT) × (columns of W)
Each entry Z[i][j]= (row i of AT) · (column j of W) — a plain dot product
General processEvery row of AT gets dotted with every column of W, filling Z one element at a time
🎯 Real-world reading

This is exactly the mechanism behind np.matmul(A_in, W) from the vectorization lesson — each entry of the output is one dot product between a row of the (transposed) input and a column of the weight matrix. That's literally how one layer's worth of neuron computations get produced simultaneously in a single matrix multiply, instead of looping neuron by neuron.

Recap

1Dot product: multiply matching elements, sum them up.

2Same dot product can be written as aTw (row vector × column-based matrix).

3Vector × matrix: each output entry = aT dotted with one column of the matrix.

4Matrix × matrix: each output entry = (row of AT) · (column of W) — repeated for every row/column pair.
