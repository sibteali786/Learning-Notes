Electronegativity of Elements

Pauling scale — color intensity increases with electronegativity (blue = low → red = high)

0.7 (low, e.g. Cs)3.98 (high, e.g. F)
Reading the map:
  • Electronegativity increases going left → right across a period, and bottom → top up a group.
  • Fluorine (3.98) is the most electronegative element on the table — the deepest red.
  • Cesium / Francium (~0.7–0.79) are the least electronegative — deepest blue.
  • Noble gases are mostly left uncolored/gray — they rarely form bonds, so EN isn't meaningfully defined for most of them.
  • To find ΔEN for a bond, just subtract the two values — bigger gap = more polar/ionic; near-zero gap between two low-EN metals = metallic.
