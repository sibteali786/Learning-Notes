Bond Length & Potential Energy Curve — H₂

Why do the two atoms in diatomic hydrogen settle at exactly 74 pm apart? D3.js curve

Problem Statement

A pure sample of hydrogen doesn't stay as lone H atoms — most pair up into H₂ (diatomic hydrogen), sharing their single valence electrons in a covalent bond (H–H).

At standard temperature & pressure, the nuclei settle at a very specific separation: ≈74 picometers. The question: why that distance, and not closer or farther?

74 pm
equilibrium bond length
−432 kJ/mol
potential energy at equilibrium (= bond energy)

How It Works

Plot potential energy (kJ/mol) vs. internuclear distance (pm). Two competing effects shape the curve:

  • PULL APART → Coulomb attraction is lost → PE rises, asymptoting to 0 at infinite separation.
  • SQUEEZE TOGETHER → nucleus–nucleus & electron–electron repulsion dominate → PE rises steeply.
  • EQUILIBRIUM → the minimum of the curve, at 74 pm — the stable, "just right" distance.
💡 Spring analogy: Think of the bond like a spring — pull it apart (add energy), squeeze it in (add energy). The minimum is the spring's natural resting length. This curve is a real Morse potential, V(r) = D₀(1−e−a(r−r₀))² − D₀, fit to H₂'s actual r₀ = 74 pm and D₀ = 432 kJ/mol — so the curvature is physically accurate, not hand-drawn.

⚠️ Note: Bond energy grows with smaller atoms and higher bond order (single → double → triple). This graph is diatomic hydrogen specifically — every bond has its own unique curve/minimum.

Real-World Example Scenario

🧪 Scenario: Imagine you're an engineer designing hydrogen storage for fuel cells. To crack H₂ into free H atoms, you'd need to supply at least 432 kJ per mole of H₂ — the depth of this potential well. Less energy, and the molecule just vibrates around the 74 pm minimum without breaking apart; more, and you overcome the well and separate the atoms for good.

Practical / Interview Tips

  • The curve's minimum = equilibrium bond length; its depth = bond energy.
  • Zero PE is a convention — "atoms infinitely far apart," not an absolute zero.
  • Left of the minimum = repulsion dominates (steep wall); right = attraction dominates (gentle asymptote toward 0).
  • "Why stable at 74 pm and not 0 pm?" → going below 74 pm costs energy (nuclear/electron repulsion), so 74 pm is the lowest-energy arrangement.
