SCN⁻ — Resonance & Formal Charge

Thiocyanate anion: three resonance structures, one dominant contributor
Problem
SCN⁻ has 3 valid resonance structures (S≡C–N, S=C=N, S–C≡N), each moving the triple bond to a different position. Formal charge tells you which one is closest to how the real molecule actually behaves — the same tool used earlier for NO₃⁻ and HNO₂, now applied to pick a winner among three very different-looking structures.

Which Structure Wins — and Why

Step 1: Minimize nonzero formal charges

Structure 1 (+1/0/−2) has the largest spread — rule it out first. That leaves Structures 2 and 3, both with only one nonzero atom.

Step 2: Put negative FC on the more electronegative atom

Structure 2 puts the −1 on N (EN = 3.0). Structure 3 puts the −1 on S (EN = 2.5). Since nitrogen is more electronegative than sulfur, it's more "comfortable" holding extra electron density — Structure 2 is the dominant resonance contributor.

Real-World Example Scenarios
Scenario 1 — Ambidentate ligand chemistry: Because SCN⁻'s electron density is concentrated more on N in the dominant structure, it typically bonds to metal centers through nitrogen (forming "isothiocyanate" complexes) rather than through sulfur — a direct real-world consequence of the formal-charge analysis here, well known in coordination chemistry.
Scenario 2 — Qualitative analysis / forensic tests: Thiocyanate's reaction with Fe³⁺ to form the deep blood-red [Fe(SCN)]²⁺ complex is a classic qualitative test for iron — the SCN⁻ ion's actual reactive geometry (informed by which resonance form dominates) is why this specific color-forming reaction happens the way it does.
Interview / Practical Tips
→ Don't stop at "fewest nonzero charges" if there's a tie — that's only the first filter; always apply the electronegativity tiebreaker next.
→ Sum of formal charges must equal the ion's overall charge in every structure — quick check: 0+0−1 = −1 ✓ for Structure 2, confirming the arithmetic.
→ "Dominant contributor" doesn't mean the other two structures don't exist — the real resonance hybrid still blends all three, just weighted more toward Structure 2.
→ This exact two-step ranking process (minimize charge spread, then place negative charge on the most electronegative atom) is the standard way exam questions ask you to compare resonance structures — memorize the order, not just the individual rules.
