Empirical Formula from Combustion Analysis

Worked example — CₓHₜ combustion → CO₂ + H₂O

Problem What Are We Solving?

A compound made only of carbon and hydrogen is completely combusted. We're told the mass of CO₂ and H₂O produced — 5.65 g CO₂ and 3.47 g H₂O — and asked to find the compound's empirical formula.
CxHy + O₂ → CO₂ + H₂O

Key insight: all the carbon in the original compound ends up in the CO₂, and all the hydrogen ends up in the H₂O — so we can trace atoms backward through the products.

Formula Step-by-Step Calculation

Moles of C (from CO₂)
5.65 g CO₂ × (1 mol CO₂ / 44.01 g) × (1 mol C / 1 mol CO₂) ≈ 0.128 mol C

1:1 ratio — each CO₂ molecule has exactly 1 carbon.

Moles of H (from H₂O)
3.47 g H₂O × (1 mol H₂O / 18.016 g) × (2 mol H / 1 mol H₂O) ≈ 0.385 mol H

2:1 ratio — each H₂O molecule has 2 hydrogens, so multiply by 2.

Find the ratio
H : C = 0.385 / 0.128 ≈ 3

For every 1 carbon, there are 3 hydrogens.

Result

The empirical formula is CH₃.

Tips Practical Tips

Tip In combustion analysis of CₓHᵧ compounds, all C ends up in CO₂ and all H ends up in H₂O — that's the whole trick that lets you work backward.
Tip Always multiply by the atom count per product molecule (1 C per CO₂, 2 H per H₂O) — forgetting this factor is the most common mistake.
Tip If oxygen is also present in the original compound, you can't measure it directly this way — instead find it by subtracting the mass of C and H (from moles × molar mass) from the original sample's total mass.
