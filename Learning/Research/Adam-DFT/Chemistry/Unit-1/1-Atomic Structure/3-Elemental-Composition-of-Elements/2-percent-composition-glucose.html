Percent Composition by Mass

Worked example — % Carbon in Glucose (C₆H₁₂O₆)

Problem What Are We Solving?

Given only the molecular formula of glucose (C₆H₁₂O₆) and the average atomic masses of C, H, and O — figure out what percentage of glucose's total mass comes from carbon alone. The actual sample size doesn't matter; the percentage is the same whether you have a grain or a mountain of glucose, so we assume 1 mole to make the math clean.

Core The Setup

ElementAtoms per glucose moleculeAverage atomic mass (u)Molar mass (g/mol)
Carbon (C)612.0112.01
Hydrogen (H)121.0081.008
Oxygen (O)616.0016.00

Assuming 1 mole of glucose → 6 mol C, 12 mol H, 6 mol O (ratios scale directly from the formula subscripts).

Formula % by Mass = Part ÷ Whole

% C by mass = (mass of C in sample) / (mass of 1 mol glucose) × 100
= (6 mol C × 12.01 g/mol) / [(6 mol C × 12.01 g/mol) + (12 mol H × 1.008 g/mol) + (6 mol O × 16.00 g/mol)]
Step by step

Numerator: 6 × 12.01 = 72.06 g

Denominator: 72.06 + (12 × 1.008 = 12.096) + (6 × 16.00 = 96.00) = 180.156 g

72.06 ÷ 180.156 = 0.4000 → 40.00% carbon by mass

Rule Significant Figures — How to Decide

The rule to remember

Your final answer can never have more significant figures than your least precise input measurement. Every intermediate step should carry extra digits (don't round early), but the moment you report a final answer, round it down to match your least precise starting value.

  • Multiplication/division: result's sig figs = the sig figs of the input with the fewest sig figs.
  • Addition/subtraction: result's decimal places = the input with the fewest decimal places (not sig figs directly).
  • In this problem: 12.01, 1.008, and 16.00 all have 4 significant figures — that's the tightest constraint anywhere in the calculation, so the final answer is capped at 4 sig figs: 40.00%.
Memory trick

Think of sig figs like a weakest-link chain — your answer's precision can never be stronger than the least precise number you multiplied or divided by. Look at every input, find the one with the fewest sig figs, and that number decides your final answer's precision.

Tips Practical Tips

Tip Don't round intermediate values (like 72.06 or 180.156) — only round the very final answer, or errors compound.
Tip Percent composition is independent of sample size — always safe to assume "1 mole" to simplify arithmetic.
Tip Units should fully cancel (grams/grams) leaving a pure percentage — if they don't cancel, you've set up the ratio wrong.
