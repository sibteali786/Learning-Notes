4. Average Atomic Mass = Weighted Average

average atomic mass = Σ (relative abundance × atomic mass)
Example — Chlorine:
Cl-35 (34.97 u, 75.76%) + Cl-37 (36.97 u, 24.24%)
= (0.7576 × 34.97) + (0.2424 × 36.97) = 26.49 + 8.96 = 35.45 u ✅ matches periodic table

Rule of thumbThe more abundant isotope pulls the average closer to its own mass.

Lighter ions / higher charge → deflected more. Heavier ions / lower charge → deflected less.

📌 Summary

Isotopes = same protons, different neutrons
Isotopes → different atomic masses
Relative abundance = % of a specific isotope in nature
Average atomic mass = weighted average of isotope masses
Mass spectrometry measures relative abundance experimentally
Spectrometer sorts ions by mass-to-charge ratio (m/z)
Spectrum: y-axis = abundance, x-axis = m/z (or mass if z=1)
Periodic table masses = relative/average atomic masses, not mass numbers
