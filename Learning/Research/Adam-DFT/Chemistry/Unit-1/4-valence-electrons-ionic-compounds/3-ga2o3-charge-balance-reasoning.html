Why "Cross the Charges" Actually Works

The Ions

The Real Rule: Net Charge = 0

Total positive charge must exactly cancel total negative charge. So find the smallest common multiple of 3 and 2 → that's 6.

  • Reach +6 using Ga³⁺ → need 2 Ga (2 × 3 = 6)
  • Reach −6 using O²⁻ → need 3 O (3 × 2 = 6)
2 Ga³⁺ + 3 O²⁻ → (+6) + (−6) = 0 → Ga₂O₃

Why the "Cross" Shortcut Works

Crossing charges as subscripts (charge of one ion → subscript of the other) is just a fast way of forcing both sides to hit that same common-multiple number. It works cleanly whenever the two charge magnitudes don't share a common factor — like 3 and 2.

If the charges do share a factor (e.g. Ca²⁺ and O²⁻), crossing gives Ca₂O₂ — but that simplifies down to CaO. Always reduce to the smallest whole-number ratio.
