GROUPS VALENCE ELECTRONS OCTET RULE IONIC COMPOUNDS

📌 Problem Statement

Why do elements with wildly different atomic numbers (Li, Na, K) behave almost identically? Why does the periodic table's column matter more than its row for predicting reactivity?

Because elements in the same group share the same number of valence electrons — and valence electrons are the reactive electrons that determine what ion an atom forms and what ionic compounds it builds.

⚙️ How It Works

1. The Octet Rule

Atoms are most stable with a full outer shell — generally 8 valence electrons.

Exception: the 1st shell is full with just 2 electrons — that's why He is stable with only 2.

Proof: Group 18 (noble gases) already have full outer shells → extremely unreactive. He (shell 1, full), Ne (shell 2, full), Ar (shell 3, full)...

2. Two Paths to "Full": Lose or Gain

An atom takes whichever path needs fewer electrons moved to reach a noble-gas configuration.

GroupValence e⁻Easier PathResultCharge
1 — Alkali metals1Lose 1 (vs. gain 7)Cation+1
2 — Alkaline earth metals2Lose 2 (vs. gain 6)Cation+2
16 — Oxygen group6Gain 2 (vs. lose 6)Anion−2
17 — Halogens7Gain 1 (vs. lose 7)Anion−1
18 — Noble gases8 (or 2)Already fullUnreactive0
Hydrogen is the odd one out: it needs only 1 more electron to match He, so it can either lose 1 (→ H⁺) or gain 1 (→ H⁻).

3. SVG: Electron Transfer in Ionic Bonding

Li gives its 1 valence electron to Cl, which needs only 1 more to complete its octet. Li becomes Li⁺, Cl becomes Cl⁻ — opposite charges attract → ionic bond.

Predicting the Formula: Balance the Charges

Ionic compounds are always neutral overall — total positive charge = total negative charge.

Ca²⁺ + 2(I¹⁻) → CaI₂
Ca²⁺ + O²⁻ → CaO
Quick method: cross the charge magnitudes as subscripts. Ca (charge 2) + I (charge 1) → subscript of I becomes 2 → CaI₂.

🎯 Practical / Exam Tips

  • Given a group number, instantly get valence electrons: Group 1 → 1, Group 2 → 2, Group 16 → 6, Group 17 → 7.
  • Charge an element forms = the "shorter path" to 8. Groups 1–2 lose; groups 16–17 gain.
  • To write any ionic formula fast: cross the charge numbers as subscripts, then simplify if they share a common factor.
  • Noble gases (Group 18) form ions essentially never — full shell already.
  • Hydrogen is the exception to memorize separately — can be H⁺ or H⁻.
