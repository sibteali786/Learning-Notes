Writing Ionic Formulas — Calcium Bromide

AP Chemistry · Unit 1 · Naming & charge-balancing ionic compounds

CATION FIRST CHARGE BALANCE CROSS-DOWN METHOD

📌 Problem Statement

Given just a compound's name — "calcium bromide" — how do you figure out the actual chemical formula (CaBr₂) without memorizing it?

Answer: identify each ion's charge from its group location, then balance charges so the compound is neutral overall.

⚙️ How It Works — 3 Steps

Step 1 — Naming order tells you the cation

Convention: the positive ion (cation) is always named/written first. "Calcium bromide" → calcium is the cation, bromide is the anion.

Step 2 — Get each charge from group number

ElementGroupTendencyIon
Calcium (Ca)2 (alkaline earth)Loses 2 e⁻Ca²⁺
Bromine (Br)17 (halogen)Gains 1 e⁻Br¹⁻

Step 3 — Cross the charges to balance

Charges must cancel to zero net charge. Ca²⁺ needs two Br¹⁻ to balance out (2+ and 2×1− = 2−).

Ca²⁺ + 2 Br¹⁻ → CaBr₂
Shortcut: the numeric value of one ion's charge becomes the subscript of the other ion. Ca is 2+ → Br gets subscript 2. Br is 1− → Ca gets subscript 1 (omitted).

🧪 Example Scenario

Try aluminum oxide: Al is group 13 → Al³⁺. Oxygen is group 16 → O²⁻.

Cross the charges: Al gets subscript 2, O gets subscript 3.

Al₂O₃
This cross-down method works for any main-group cation/anion pair — same logic used for NaCl, MgO, CaI₂, AlCl₃, etc.

🎯 Exam Tips

  • Cation always written/named first — don't second-guess the order.
  • Get charge magnitude from group #, not memorization: Groups 1/2/13 → +1/+2/+3; Groups 15/16/17 → −3/−2/−1.
  • Cross-down trick: charge magnitude of one ion → subscript of the other. Simplify if both subscripts share a common factor (e.g. Ca²⁺O²⁻ → CaO, not Ca₂O₂).
  • Never write a subscript of 1 — it's implied.
