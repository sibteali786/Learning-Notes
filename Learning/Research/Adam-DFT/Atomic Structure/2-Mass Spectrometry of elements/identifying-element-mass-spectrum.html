Identifying an Element from Its Mass Spectrum

Worked example — Khan Academy video transcript

Problem What Are We Solving?

Given a mystery pure element's mass spectrum (a set of peaks showing isotope atomic masses and their relative abundances), calculate the average atomic mass and use it to identify the element on the periodic table — without needing to know the element's identity up front.

Data The Given Spectrum

Isotope abundance82%7%10%1%
Atomic mass (u)88878684

Percentages sum to 100% (82+7+10+1).

Diagram Reading the Spectrum

Peak height = relative abundance. The 88 u peak towers over the rest → dominates the average.

Formula Weighted Average Calculation

avg atomic mass = Σ (relative abundance × atomic mass)
= (0.82 × 88) + (0.07 × 87) + (0.10 × 86) + (0.01 × 84)

You don't even need a calculator to get a good estimate — see the shortcut below.

Shortcut Estimating Without a Calculator

Reasoning

82% of the sample sits at exactly 88 u — the heaviest weight by far. The other isotopes (87, 86, 84) are all lighter, so they can only pull the average down, never up. So the answer must be a little less than 88, never equal to or above it.

Takeaway

The mystery element is Strontium (Sr) — identified purely by comparing the weighted-average estimate against known periodic table values, without ever running the full decimal calculation.

Tips Practical Tips

Tip The average atomic mass always lands closest to whichever isotope has the highest abundance — use that as your first sanity check.
Tip Rule out candidate elements whose mass is higher than your heaviest observed isotope — the weighted average can never exceed the maximum isotope mass.
Tip Estimating before calculating catches arithmetic mistakes fast — if your final number doesn't land near the dominant peak, recheck your work.
